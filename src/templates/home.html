How Real Estate Closings Typically Work in Arizona

Arizona is generally described as an escrow state. In most residential sales, a licensed escrow agent, often the escrow division of a title company, holds the earnest money, follows written instructions from both sides, prepares the settlement statement, and disburses funds once the conditions are met. Attorneys can be part of a deal, but an attorney-led closing is not typically required the way it is in some other states.

A wholesale double close adds a second layer to that process. The A-to-B and B-to-C sides each need their own instructions, title work, and settlement figures, and the escrow officer typically needs the purchase side funded before the resale side can close and pay out. That is why double-closing funding in Phoenix depends on everyone working from the same documents, including any HOA disclosures the property needs. The escrow or title company handling your file can confirm the current requirements for your transaction.

Frequently Asked Questions About Double-Closing Funding in Phoenix

Most questions about double-closing funding in Phoenix come down to what the contracts say, how the resale to your end buyer is structured, and how the escrow or title company plans to handle both sides. The answers below explain how those pieces usually affect a funding request here. They stay general because every deal has its own documents and every closing office has its own procedures. For anything specific to your file, the escrow officer or closing professional can confirm current requirements, and you can send us the deal itself for a transaction-level review of the contracts and closing plan.

Why does a double close in Arizona usually run through escrow instead of an attorney's office?

Arizona closings are typically handled by licensed escrow agents, often working within title companies, rather than by attorneys. For a double close, the escrow officer usually manages the instructions, title work, and settlement statements for both the A-to-B and B-to-C sides. You can still hire an attorney for advice. Ask the escrow company early how it handles back-to-back closings, since procedures and internal policies can differ from one company to the next.

Can the two sides of a Phoenix deal close with different title companies?

Sometimes, depending on the transaction and what each company will agree to. Some wholesalers keep both sides with one escrow company to simplify the sequence, while others have an end buyer who wants to use their own title company. When two title or closing companies are involved, coordination takes more steps, and our schedule lists a 1.75% fee for that setup. Tell us the arrangement when you submit so the review reflects it.

What changes when the Phoenix property is in an HOA?

Mostly the paperwork. Arizona law requires certain association disclosure information to be delivered to a purchaser in a planned community, and escrow will typically request payoff or demand figures from the association. In a double close, those items may touch both sides of the transaction. Let us know whether the property has an HOA, and share any association documents you already have, so we can account for them when we review the request.

Does an older Phoenix house call for different documents than a newer subdivision home?

It can. Older single-family homes may come with deferred repairs, and the end buyer's contract might include inspection terms or credits that change the resale price. Newer subdivision homes are more likely to involve HOA items. In both cases we look at the two contracts to see how property condition shows up in the numbers, so include any repair notes, addenda, or inspection terms that apply to your deal.

How soon can funding be available for a Phoenix double close?

Timing depends on the transaction documents, funding needs, and closing coordination. No specific closing time is promised. A deal with both signed contracts, a confirmed end buyer, and an escrow officer who already has instructions for both sides is easier to review than one still missing pieces. Sending complete documents with your first submission is the most practical way to avoid delays.

Is my personal income or credit part of the review?

Documentation requirements vary by transaction. Do not assume any credit or income requirement is waived. Our attention goes to the property, the purchase and resale contracts, the end buyer, and the closing structure. If a deal has unusual terms, we may ask for more information about the transaction or the parties involved before it moves forward.

How does transactional capital move through an A-to-B and B-to-C closing?

It funds the first closing so the second one can happen. Transactional funding provides money to buy the property from the original seller, which is the A-to-B side. Your end buyer then purchases from you on the B-to-C side, and proceeds from that sale typically repay the funding along with the applicable fee. In Arizona, the escrow instructions usually spell out how and when each set of funds moves.
